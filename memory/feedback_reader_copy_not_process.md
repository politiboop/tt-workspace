On 2026-10-26 the user pasted The Count's cautions screen back and asked "Why do we have this?" It printed the prologue sweep's gaps and cautions verbatim: entry ids, "beats", "verifier", "critic", "prior outline", "the reader's second example", dated update brackets. It existed because the approval had said to keep the gaps and cautions visible, taken literally.

**Why:** The caveats are the honest part of the page and must stay, but a reader cannot use build documentation; process vocabulary reads as noise and hides the few things that matter to weighing the record (inferred reasons, stand-in dates, cut quotations, alleged matters).

**How to apply:** Every reader-facing surface gets copy written for readers. Where the copy must stay faithful to research notes, generate it from the notes with an agent under a no-new-claims rule and have a second agent trace each line back; never hand-write factual lines. Put each caution on the step it concerns. Keep the raw notes on the page behind a disclosure (and in the data file) for anyone checking the work. Treat ids, "beat", "verifier", "critic", "sweep", "prior outline" and "the reader" as banned on screen. Related: [[the-count-prototype]], [[feedback-story-first]], [[feedback-design-preferences]].
